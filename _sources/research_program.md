<p class="research-intro">The research is designed as one connected scale-up experiment. Evidence is first measured under controlled field conditions, then tested under production-scale conditions, and finally translated to watershed and coastal consequences. Use the pathway below to move directly between the three research scales.</p>

## Task 1 — Lysimeter Scale

<div class="research-story research-story-left">
  <div class="research-story-visual">
    <img src="_static/img/task1_treatments.png" alt="Conceptual illustration of the Task 1 lysimeter treatments">
  </div>
  <div class="research-story-copy">
    <p class="story-kicker">MEASURE THE MECHANISM</p>
    <p class="story-question">Can humic acid reduce nitrogen and phosphorus movement below the tomato root zone while supporting crop production?</p>
    <div class="process-ribbon" aria-label="Task 1 research process">
      <span>Four treatments</span><i>→</i><span>Suction-plate leachate + soil sensors</span><i>→</i><span>Nutrient-loss comparison</span>
    </div>
    <p>Three replicates are used for a background control, conventional 100% fertilizer, HA with 100% fertilizer, and HA with 50% fertilizer. Leachate chemistry is interpreted together with soil-water conditions, rainfall, irrigation, and crop response to quantify how HA changes nutrient retention and leaching.</p>
    <p class="measure-line"><strong>Core evidence:</strong> TN & TP • nitrate/nitrite • ammonia • soluble reactive phosphorus • soil water • drainage • crop response</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Task 2 — Field Scale

<div class="research-story research-story-right">
  <div class="research-story-copy">
    <p class="story-kicker">VALIDATE IN PRODUCTION</p>
    <p class="story-question">Does the nutrient-reduction strategy remain effective under field-scale tomato production while maintaining crop performance and economic feasibility?</p>
    <div class="process-ribbon" aria-label="Task 2 research process">
      <span>Conventional practice</span><i>↔</i><span>HA + reduced nutrients</span><i>→</i><span>Water + crop + economic response</span>
    </div>
    <p>The field experiment at UF/IFAS SWFREC compares standard nutrient management with HA under reduced nutrient input. Soil and shallow-groundwater observations are combined with UAV hyperspectral imagery, tomato yield and fruit-quality measurements, and farm-level economic analysis.</p>
    <p class="measure-line"><strong>Core evidence:</strong> soil condition • shallow groundwater • UAV crop health • yield & quality • fertilizer savings • return on investment</p>
  </div>

</div>

## Task 3 — Watershed & Coast

<div class="research-story research-story-left">
  <div class="research-story-visual">
    <img src="_static/img/task3_modeling.png" alt="Conceptual illustration of Task 3 watershed modeling, red-tide analysis, and decision support">
  </div>
  <div class="research-story-copy">
    <p class="story-kicker">SCALE THE ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACT</p>
    <p class="story-question">If measured nutrient losses decline at the lysimeter and field scales, what could broader adoption mean for Peace River nutrient loads and downstream red-tide risk?</p>
    <div class="task3-chain" aria-label="Task 3 modeling chain">
      <span><b>3a</b> WAM watershed scenarios</span><i>→</i><span><b>3b</b> <em>K. brevis</em> machine learning</span><i>→</i><span><b>3c</b> AI/LLM decision support</span>
    </div>
    <p>Qualified results from Tasks 1 and 2 are used to represent HA adoption in the Peace River Watershed Assessment Model. Resulting nutrient-load scenarios are then evaluated with the existing red-tide machine-learning framework, while the decision-support component translates the combined agronomic, economic, watershed, and coastal information into practical guidance.</p>
    <p class="measure-line"><strong>Core evidence:</strong> TN & TP load change • sensitivity & uncertainty • bloom probability/frequency/severity • stakeholder-ready guidance</p>
  </div>
</div>
